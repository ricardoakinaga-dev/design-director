# Banner and hero design

## Communication hierarchy

Every banner should answer, in order: what is this, why now/why me, and what can I do next? Define one primary message, one supporting detail, and one primary action. Do not cram a landing page into a banner.

## Composition and crop

Choose subject placement, safe text zone, focal point, contrast, and crop per output ratio. Keep faces, products, logos, and essential visual evidence away from unpredictable crop edges. Specify desktop/mobile art direction when the crop cannot be shared.

## Text and CTA

Keep exact copy in HTML/SVG when it must be readable, localized, or accessible. If a raster contains text, record it verbatim and inspect it at final display size. Avoid invented claims, fake urgency, unsupported discounts, and multiple competing CTAs.

## Asset variants

Generate or select imagery for a defined role: background, subject, product, texture, or illustration. Keep identity and campaign palette stable while testing crop, subject scale, and tone. Name outputs by role and ratio, for example `vet-campaign-hero-wide.webp`, not by a random generation ID.

## QA

Check the banner at native and downscaled sizes, with real copy, dark/light surroundings, slow loading, reduced motion, and narrow crops. Confirm the action remains reachable and the image does not reduce contrast or performance.
