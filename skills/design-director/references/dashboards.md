# Dashboards and SaaS surfaces

## Start with decisions

Map the user's recurring tasks: scan, compare, filter, inspect, act, export, or recover. Set hierarchy for status, primary metric, trend, detail, and action. A dashboard is not a collection of decorative cards.

## Density with structure

Use consistent columns, labels, units, timestamps, sorting, and alignment. Tables are often the right medium for comparison; do not convert them into cards merely to appear modern. Reserve color and elevation for meaningful state or ownership.

## States and controls

Design real loading, empty, error, stale, permission, selected, filtered, paginated, and success states. Filters, tabs, sorting, forms, and primary actions must update real local state or connect to the product contract. Never present fake metrics as product truth.

## Responsive behavior

Define which columns remain, collapse, scroll, summarize, or move into detail views. Keep the primary action and status visible at narrow widths. Test keyboard navigation, focus restoration after drawers/modals, and touch-friendly controls.

## Visual language

Use restrained surfaces, clear grouping, and a compact but readable type scale. Make the product's domain vocabulary and data shape visible in the design. Avoid generic purple/blue SaaS styling, card-in-card nesting, and decorative charts with no decision value.
